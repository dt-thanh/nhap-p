# La Pura — AHP absorption ranking preparation

Generated by `scripts/lapura_ahp_prep.py`, writing to `datasets/processed_data/`.
Re-run with:

```bash
venv/bin/python scripts/lapura_ahp_prep.py
```

Deterministic and interpretable end to end: AHP + weighted scoring only. No
ML/DL, no predictive model. Source CSVs in `data/Real_estate/data/` are read
only — the script SHA-256s them before and after and aborts if they differ.
AHP weight derivation reuses `src/ranking/ahp.py` (RGMM, λmax, CI, CR).

**Weights and expert mappings in this run are domain-expert judgments, not the
spec's placeholder template.** They are attributable assumptions and still
require the team's human-in-the-loop sign-off before any recommendation built on
them is treated as final.

### `price_per_sqm_*` uses the wrong price base

The source `price_per_sqm_gross_vnd` and `price_per_sqm_net_vnd` are computed
from **`loan_price_vnd`**, not `standard_price_vnd`. Exact, not approximate: all
392 rows reconcile to the loan base within 1 VND, while the standard base is off
by up to 6.7M VND/m².

Recalculated per spec Step 5, with source values kept as
`price_per_sqm_gross_vnd_source` / `price_per_sqm_net_vnd_source`. Beyond the
spec, the loan-based figure double-counts the loan premium, which is already a
separate criterion. `area_efficiency_ratio` and `loan_premium_pct` reconcile to
rounding tolerance (4.9e-05, 3.0e-08) and are unaffected.

## 2. Key EDA insights

Descriptive. No causal claim, and the outcome columns involved are synthetic.

- **Absorption in the VN primary market is non-monotonic in price.** Buyers shop
  to a total budget ("dưới 3.5 tỷ"), not to a price per m². Realised take-up by
  ticket band: 3.0–3.5bn **61%** sold+reserved, 3.5–4bn 51%, 4–5bn 50%, 2.5–3bn
  43%, then a cliff to **5–7bn 36%**. Demand depth peaks in the mortgage-supported
  middle and thins sharply above it.
- **The mid-floor price premium was a mix artifact — resolved.** Raw medians
  showed mid floors priciest per m² (65.8M vs 59.3M high). Cause: all 39 Loft
  units, the most expensive type per m², sit exclusively in mid floors. Adjusting
  for type, the normal ordering returns — within-type z-score medians are high
  +0.058, low +0.045, mid −0.041, and within 2PN alone high 57.3M > low 56.6M >
  mid 56.2M. The high > mid > low mapping stands.
- **But floor barely moves price here.** The within-2PN high-to-mid spread is
  ~1.9%, against the 10–20% stacking differential typical of a real VN launch.
  Position is a weak signal in this dataset, which is why it carries 13.3% rather
  than the template's 20%.
- **Product mix is concentrated.** 2PN is 152/392 units (39%) and has the lowest
  median price/m² (56.7M) — the deepest segment sits at the best value.
- **Subdivision separates units weakly**: median price/m² 57.5M (Lusso d'Arte) to
  64.5M (Risa), a ~12% band.
- **Area efficiency is nearly constant** (0.872–0.927, ~5.5pp) and is
  uncorrelated with everything else (|ρ| ≤ 0.23). It cannot carry real weight.
- **`bedrooms` and `bathrooms` are the same variable.** Identical in 328/392 rows
  (84%), Spearman 0.70 on the deviation measures. Scoring both double-counts.
- **`loan_premium_pct` is not continuous — it is subdivision in disguise.** It
  takes exactly three values, each mapping 1:1 onto a subdivision: Zenia 1.7%
  (158 units), Risa 4.1% (114), Lusso Saigon + Lusso d'Arte 9.8% (120). See
  `eda_loan_premium_distribution.csv` and limitation 9.
- **Area efficiency is tightly unimodal**: 81% of units fall in 0.89–0.92, and
  the extremes are thin (10 units below 0.88, 8 above 0.92 — the latter being
  large Garden units at a 103m² median). Confirms the demotion to 3.2% weight.
  See `eda_area_efficiency_distribution.csv`.

### Changes from the spec's 35/30/20/15 template, and why

1. **`ticket_depth_score` added (29%).** Total ticket price is the binding
   constraint on absorption. It is deliberately non-monotonic — a step curve
   peaking at 3.0–3.5bn and falling to 40/100 above 7bn. A min-max cost criterion
   on price/m² cannot express this shape, which is the main reason the template
   ranking barely distinguished fast from slow inventory.
2. **`segment_depth_score` added, `bedrooms_score` removed.** Bedrooms *define*
   the segment, so scoring both counts the same fact twice.
3. **`bathrooms_score` dropped** — 84% identical to bedrooms.
4. **`balcony_direction_score` dropped** — an independent synthetic draw stacked
   on an already-synthetic orientation signal, inflating synthetic exposure for
   no information.
5. **`area_efficiency` demoted to 3.2%** — a 5.5pp range cannot justify 7%+.
6. **Physical Attractiveness cut 15% → 9.3%.** Every input in this group is
   fabricated. Handing 15% of the decision to synthetic data is a defect, not a
   neutral default.
7. **Position cut 20% → 13.3%** — see the weak floor-price signal above.
8. **`subdivision`, `tower`, `unit_number` remain unscored.** No validated
   ordering exists; they stay descriptive/join columns.

### Consistency — read before quoting CR

| Matrix | n | CR | Saaty threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| goal: absorption potential | 4 | **0.00154** | 0.08 |
| Affordability & Demand Depth | 3 | 0.00000 | 0.05 |
| Product Fit | 3 | **0.01577** | 0.05 |
| Position | 1 | — | single criterion |
| Physical Attractiveness | 3 | 0.00000 | 0.05 |

All pass, and unlike the previous template run these are **real** numbers — the
judgments were given independently rather than back-derived from target weights.
Two caveats, stated plainly:

- With **n = 3** there are only three judgments, so consistency reduces to one
  arithmetic identity (`a₁₃ = a₁₂ × a₂₃`). CR = 0 there means only that a triad
  was not self-contradictory. It is a weak test.
- The **n = 4 goal matrix** is the only genuine multi-constraint check. CR =
  0.00154 against a 0.08 threshold, with the largest hotspot at
  Affordability vs Physical (judged 5.00, implied 5.48).

### Validation (`validation_sensitivity.csv`)

- **Sensitivity**: ±5%/±10% on each group weight gives minimum Spearman
  **0.9991**, minimum Kendall τ-b 0.977, worst top-50 overlap 47/50. Stable.
- **Equal-weight baseline**: Spearman 0.8930, Kendall τ-b 0.7104, top-50 overlap
  **33/50** — the weighting does real work where the sales team acts.
- **Versus the spec template ranking**: Spearman 0.9195, top-50 overlap 36/50,
  106 units moving more than 50 places. The expert revision is a material change,
  not a cosmetic one.
- **Expert-ranking comparison: still NOT RUN.** No expert-labelled ranking exists
  in the source. My judgments set the weights; they are not an independent
  ranking to correlate against. This remains the main open validation gap.

## 4. Limitations

1. **The improvement against actual outcomes is small, and partly circular.**
   P(a sold unit outscores an available unit) is 0.576, up from 0.565 under the
   template — where 0.50 is no signal. I inspected realised take-up by ticket
   band while designing the demand-depth curve, so this comparison is not an
   independent test. It should not be quoted as validation. The curve's *shape*
   comes from market knowledge; the dataset is merely consistent with it.
2. **The CRM outcomes are synthetic anyway** (`crm_signals_origin =
   synthetic_v1_causal_funnel`), generated by a funnel model whose drivers are
   unknown to this pipeline. Agreement or disagreement with them is weak evidence
   in either direction.
3. **Treat the output as tiers, not as a strict order.** The top 15 units are
   near-identical B3 2PN high-floor stack units scoring 93.88 down to 91.94.
   Ordering within such a cluster is arithmetic noise; "rank 1 vs rank 14" is a
   distinction without a difference. Use `score_band`, and let the agent explain
   the tier rather than defend a position.
4. **This is a priority list, not a write-off list.** All 56 3PN units land in
   Low or Very Low, driven by a ~5.2bn median ticket. That is a correct read of
   relative absorption speed, but the team still has to sell them — the ranking
   sequences effort, it does not condemn inventory.
5. **`view`, `direction`, `corner_unit_proxy` are SYNTHETIC**
   (`physical_features_origin = synthetic_v1_tower_stack_floor_band`), carrying
   9.3% total weight, flagged as such in every output.
6. **`agency_name` is synthetic**; **`data_profile = 'demo'` for all 392 units**
   — this is not production CRM data.
7. **`ticket_depth` and `segment_depth` cut-offs are my judgment.** The band
   edges (2.5/3/3.5/4/5/7bn) and segment scores encode the Bình Dương / HCMC
   satellite market at 2025 pricing. They travel poorly to another project or
   another price cycle and should be re-elicited per launch.
9. **`loan_premium_score` reintroduces subdivision through the back door.** I
   excluded `subdivision` from the hierarchy because no expert-validated ordering
   exists — but loan premium is a 3-level developer policy tier that is perfectly
   determined by subdivision, so scoring it at 7.26% imposes the implicit
   ordering Zenia > Risa > Lusso. I kept it because a buyer paying 9.8% more to
   use the loan package genuinely faces worse affordability: that is an observed
   price fact, categorically different from inventing a prestige ranking. But the
   team must decide knowingly, since ranking with it means ranking the
   subdivisions on financing terms. Dropping it is a one-line change
   (`HIERARCHY["affordability_demand_depth"]` in `scripts/lapura_ahp_prep.py`).
10. **Score bands are now percentile-based**, not the spec's fixed 90/80/70/60
   cut-offs. Those produced zero "Very High" units and piled 46% into one bucket,
   because a weighted mean of 0–100 scores is regressive toward the middle and
   its absolute level carries no business meaning. Bands are now top 10% / next
   20% / middle 40% / next 20% / bottom 10% → 40 Very High, 78 High, 157 Medium,
   78 Low, 39 Very Low. The raw score is retained for transparency.

## 6. MiniCRM compatibility

No schema change; no primary or external ID renamed or regenerated. Every output
carries `unit_external_id` and `unit_code` and joins back through:

```
crm_projects.id → crm_areas.project_id → crm_units.area_id
```

## 7. Leakage control

Excluded from `ahp_input_clean.csv` and from every scored criterion:
`unit_status`, `is_sold`, `days_to_sell`, `reserved_at`, `sold_at`,
`lead_count`, `qualified_lead_count`, `booking_count`, `reservation_count`,
`first_lead_at`, `first_booking_at`, `days_to_first_lead`, `days_to_reserve`,
`deal_lifecycle_stage`, `has_lead`, `has_booking`, `has_reservation`,
`deal_external_id`, `deal_status`, `lost_at`.

`ticket_depth_score` and `segment_depth_score` use `standard_price_vnd` and
`unit_type_normalized`, both observed launch-time attributes — not outcomes. The
ranking represents only what the system could know at launch.
